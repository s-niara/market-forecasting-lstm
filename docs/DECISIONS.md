## Research scope and architecture
**Recorded:** research scope, 11 October 2026; notebook-centred architecture, 16 October 2026.

**Decision:** use topic 1h, Time Series Forecasting with LSTM and Feature Engineering, with NVDA as the initial case study. Notebooks provide the analysis, methodology, demonstrations, visualisations and conclusions. Python modules provide reusable technical implementations. Notebooks import and execute the actual functions, and important transformations and algorithms have one authoritative implementation. Findings inform whether those implementations should be retained or improved.

**Rationale and implications:** separating scientific reasoning from reusable algorithms supports reproducibility and technical demonstration without duplicating production logic. Each stage has one authoritative notebook with its necessary executed outputs. A later dashboard can present verified results. Ticker configuration and retraining support future extension but do not establish compatibility with every instrument. TensorFlow/Keras supplies the LSTM implementation; the verified environment is recorded separately.

## Data acquisition, integrity and temporal boundaries
**Recorded:** snapshot preservation and test reservation, 11 October 2026; bounded exploratory access, 16 October 2026.

**Decision:** preserve frozen vendor snapshots and exclude vendor data, environments and generated model artifacts from Git. Reserve 2025 for final forecast evaluation. Exploratory loading uses explicit inclusive bounds to select timestamp positions before decoding and validating quote values. Notebook 01 uses the training interval. Snapshot identity is checked by hashing opaque bytes, separately from numerical and calendar validation.

**Rationale and alternatives:** frozen inputs support reproducibility, while temporal boundaries restrict the information used in analysis. Loading complete quote arrays before filtering would interpret values outside the permitted interval. A separate notebook parsing algorithm would duplicate technical logic. Bounded loading in the reusable module avoids both issues. Shared `price_quality()` diagnostics explain the checks enforced by `validate_prices()`; independent session auditing additionally detects gaps shared across instruments.

**Constraints:** the bounded extractor supports the frozen chronological Yahoo scalar-array schema and cannot certify ordering or completeness in an unread future tail. Values and exchange-local dates are preserved without silent adjustment, filling or sorting. Administrative checks do not establish forecasting skill or vendor accuracy. Raw-data sharing remains subject to permitted submission and distribution arrangements.

## 2026-10-12 — Forecast price convention
**Decision:** use Yahoo split-adjusted Close; retain dividend-adjusted Close as reference-only.

**Rationale and alternatives:** this target represents price forecasting while avoiding the mechanical discontinuity of an unadjusted split. Dividend-adjusted Close would change the economic target. Reconstructing original quotes would require separate point-in-time corporate-action handling. The chosen series is retrospective; do not apply split adjustments again or claim a point-in-time trading simulation.

## 2026-10-12 — Calendar audit and volume deferral
**Decision:** audit NASDAQ session coverage for NVDA and use the NYSE regular-session calendar as a documented proxy for SPY/XLK. Preserve half-days and exclude synthetic holiday rows. Defer volume features until adjustment semantics are independently corroborated.

**Rationale and implications:** matching instrument dates alone cannot detect a jointly missing session. The calendar-library check found no missing or unexpected dates, but does not constitute exchange certification. Insufficient evidence about volume warrants leaving the raw field unchanged and proceeding with price-only development.

## 2026-10-13 — ARIMA statistical benchmark
**Implementation verification:** 17 October 2026.
**Decision:** compare nine nonseasonal ARIMA(p,1,q) models, p and q in {0,1,2}, without drift. Select by converged training AIC, fit on 2015–2022 and update filtered state after each one-step validation forecast without refitting coefficients.

**Rationale and alternatives:** a bounded order search limits complexity and keeps order selection independent of validation errors. First differencing addresses price-level persistence as a benchmark assumption, not a demonstrated stationarity result. Fixed coefficients separate parameter estimation from observation updates. Wider searches, drift and periodic refitting remain alternatives rather than unrecorded tuning. The unchanged price target and temporal boundaries permit direct comparison with the naive baseline. [Executed results](MILESTONE_4.md) show no improvement over naive forecasting.

**Reproducible demonstration:** Notebook 03 uses explicit development bounds, snapshot provenance and a calendar preflight. It reads the original nine candidate AIC records, reconstructs only the predetermined selected ARIMA(2,1,2) fit on the 2,014 training observations, and verifies its parameters, AIC and 502 rolling predictions against the saved evidence. A trace exposes the forecast, subsequent observed target and state update while checking that coefficients remain fixed. The single training reproduction is distinct from refitting coefficients during validation. The historical experiment date and source hashes continue to identify the original run.

**Diagnostic scope and limitations:** ADF/KPSS, ACF/PACF and innovation diagnostics use training data only. First differences are inspected diagnostically, while the ARIMA fit receives price levels with d=1 to avoid double differencing. Diagnostic settings and test nulls are explicit. Initialization observations are excluded according to the fitted state-space model, and Ljung–Box tests account for the four fitted AR/MA coefficients. Nonrejection does not establish independence or stationarity; changing volatility and finite-sample assumptions limit interpretation. These checks assess the fixed benchmark and do not introduce another order search, change d, tune on validation or justify a new forecasting claim.

## 2026-10-14 — Bounded LSTM optimisation investigation
**Decision:** retain four configurations with paired seeds 42, 123 and 2026. Configuration D changes Adam learning rate from 0.001 to 0.0003 relative to A; all configurations use batch size 32. B varies hidden units and C varies post-LSTM dropout. Twelve internal-selection runs and three selected-configuration full-development fits form the planned 15-run budget.

**Rationale and implications:** one-factor contrasts isolate capacity, regularisation and optimiser step size within a common training budget. Batch-size effects and hyperparameter interactions are not investigated. The chronological 2022 holdout, early stopping and predefined selection rule are retained in the [design](MILESTONE_5_DESIGN.md). Loss histories, convergence, best epochs and stopping epochs are required evidence. Each run restores the checkpoint minimising internal-validation standardised MSE; configurations are compared by mean USD MAE. These criteria are distinct. Inference-mode fitting and validation losses use the same end-of-epoch weights with dropout disabled; minibatch loss uses evolving weights and active dropout where configured. The selected configuration and completed runs are recorded below under 14 October 2026.

## 2026-10-14 — Residual dollar-correction LSTM
**Decision:** predict a standardised next-session dollar correction and reconstruct price by adding its inverse-transformed value to the latest observed Close. Apply this formulation consistently to the Milestone 5 configurations and later feature variants.

**Rationale and alternatives:** direct-price prediction requires the recurrent network to reproduce unfamiliar price levels. A fixed persistence connection carries the known level while the network estimates departures from it. This does not remove input distribution shift or changing dollar volatility, and may underperform naive forecasting. The decision precedes new LSTM validation results; no direct-versus-residual experiment is added. The four configurations, paired seeds, 15-run budget, chronological protocol and selection rule remain unchanged.

## 15 October 2026 — Fixed feature comparison and observed outcome
Executed the approved nine-run comparison with common warm-up, training-only scaling and the selected residual LSTM settings. Models A/B/C use 1/8/10 features and 1,169/1,617/1,745 parameters. The 49-row feature warm-up removes 49 fitting examples relative to Milestone 5, so a newly fitted price-only Model A is the appropriate control. All models share 1,935 fitting examples and 502 external targets.

**Comparison scope:** equal 30-session feature sequences and fitting targets control sample eligibility and training protocol, but not effective historical context or capacity. A sequence of SMA50 values spans 79 underlying closes; recursive EMA/RSI states carry earlier history. These inputs are causal summaries, not future information. More input columns increase LSTM parameters despite fixed hidden units. Accordingly, B−A measures the complete technical representation, and C−B measures market context conditional on it; neither identifies individual feature importance. Relative MACD is algebraically dependent on the two EMA distance features. Its inclusion remains part of the fixed specification rather than an independent-information claim.

Mean MAE is 1.702600 USD for A, 1.717304 for B and 1.715305 for C. All nine runs are worse than naive on full-period MAE/RMSE. Technical features did not improve the average result; adding context offers a small, seed-dependent improvement over B but not A. No additional tuning, seed selection or individual-indicator importance claim is justified. All runs and Milestone 5 evidence are retained. The approved final comparison and refitting protocol is specified in the final-training section below. See [full evidence](MILESTONE_6.md).

## Final pre-test fitting and frozen evaluation
**Decision:** refit the frozen specifications using the complete 2015–2024 pre-test history. Retain naive persistence, ARIMA(2,1,2), primary LSTM A, technical-indicator B and context-enhanced C. Milestone 5 remains development evidence without a separate final refit. B−A and C−B retain their prespecified feature-comparison roles.

**Rationale:** refitting incorporates the most recent permissible observations while preserving model selection from development evidence. ARIMA re-estimates coefficients once with the existing no-drift specification and optimisation settings. The nine LSTMs start from fresh weights, preserve 16 units, dropout 0.2, Adam 0.001, batch 32, 19 epochs, seeds 42/123/2026 and dollar-correction reconstruction. No final-training validation split, early stopping, new search or loss-based seed selection is introduced. The frozen epoch budget is a development-selected training rule, not a claim of optimality for the larger sample.

**Eligibility and transformations:** all variants use the same 2,437 labels after the established common warm-up. Feature scalers fit 2,466 unique input rows through 2024-12-30; the 2024-12-31 Close remains a permitted fitting label and future forecast origin, but is excluded from those input-scaler rows. Indicator history retains its original 2015 initialisation. Frozen-origin transformation needs only the observed prefix and never the unknown next Close.

**Execution and freeze, 18 October 2026:** one ARIMA fit and all nine LSTM fits completed. The [manifest](../artifacts/final_training_v1/manifest.json) binds exact source/configuration/data identities, package versions, model/scaler artifacts and independent-process reload evidence. Final artifacts have their own directory; development artifacts remain unchanged. ARIMA state restoration and pre-test forecast/update checks preserve coefficients, while LSTM inference preserves weights and scalers. Pre-test checks verify computation, not independent forecasting accuracy.

**Independent evaluation:** [the frozen protocol](../configs/final_training_v1.json) specifies 2025, expected 250 exchange sessions subject to verification, and forecast → observe → update historical information. ARIMA coefficients and LSTM weights/scalers remain fixed. Report USD MAE first and RMSE second, every seed and arithmetic means of seed metrics; do not ensemble predictions. Prespecified paired contrasts are A−naive, ARIMA−naive, B−A and C−B. Positive absolute skill is baseline error minus model error; percentage skill is 100 × (1 − model error / baseline error), undefined for a zero baseline. Supplementary paired circular-block bootstrap MAE-difference intervals use 5,000 draws, seed 314159, primary block length 10 and sensitivity lengths 5/20, with 95% percentile intervals. Resample dates jointly across models/seeds; these approximate assessments depend on temporal-dependence assumptions and do not authorise selection. Representative plots use seed 42; examples use the first test target and three largest naive-error dates, shared across models.

**Evaluation execution and provenance:** the evaluation implementation imports the authoritative feature, preprocessing, inference and metric functions. All five families share one target-date sequence; each session's eleven forecasts are stored before its NVDA/SPY/XLK observations are decoded. Newly observed prices update historical context and ARIMA's filtered state, never fitted coefficients, neural weights or scaler statistics. An explicit evaluation-history limit is distinct from the unchanged development partitions. Both an inactive code-level release gate and an inactive configuration authorisation flag protect real test access.

The frozen training manifest and source archive remain immutable. The original training-source member hashes are verified against the archive, not against later evaluation source files. A separate evaluation identity records the current revision, exact source/configuration hashes and package versions. An authorised run will create one machine-readable evaluation record and a matching evaluation-source archive, refusing existing output destinations. This preserves failed-run evidence without changing the training freeze.

**Uncertainty estimand:** family MAE is the mean of seed-specific MAEs. Bootstrap calculations therefore average paired absolute losses across the fixed seeds and resample identical circular date blocks across all comparisons. This is algebraically equivalent to calculating each seed's MAE within every resample; it does not average predicted prices. The block-length sensitivity results are supplementary, not a basis for selection. Circular joins, possible regime changes, fixed rather than resampled training seeds and unadjusted multiple comparisons limit inference. Fewer observations than the largest prescribed block length cause failure rather than automatic protocol relaxation.

**Pre-test execution:** synthetic checks and a 30-session 2024 demonstration verify the complete pipeline and output format. Because the frozen parameters were fitted through these dates, their demonstration errors and intervals cannot establish independent predictive performance. The return-target alternative remains excluded, and no further fitting follows from these checks.

**Approval boundary:** model artifacts are frozen, but reserved-period access and execution remain unauthorised. The current evaluation command fails closed. Return-target experiments remain future work and are excluded from the final comparison. No Notebook 06 or 2025 price, feature, forecast or performance calculation is part of final refitting.
